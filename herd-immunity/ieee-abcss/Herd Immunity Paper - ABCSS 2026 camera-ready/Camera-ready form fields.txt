TITLE
Herd Immunity and Learning Externalities in Markets of Shared Foundation Models

AUTHORS, in the accepted order
Vignesh Nagarajan, Texas A&M University, College Station, TX, USA,
  vigneshn26@tamu.edu
Shriraghav Ashok, University of California, Berkeley, Berkeley, CA, USA,
  sashok24@berkeley.edu

ABSTRACT (275 words, LaTeX stripped)
Every firm that prices, lends or ranks with machine learning wants its model current, so it retrains on the newest data. However, that data was produced in part by its own model and by every competitor's. When many firms fine-tune the same foundation model, one firm's retraining changes what every other firm learns from next, and no firm accounts for it. This paper models that market and shows it is stable exactly when one number, the feedback reproduction number, stays below one. That number depends on how many independent models sit under the market, not on how many firms there are. When firms draw on a common pool, fifty firms built on one vendor's model behave like one learner with fifty times the footprint. On this foundation we derive three remedies. Retraining in smaller steps helps only below a critical crowding level. Correcting for feedback works like vaccination, and the share of firms that must correct follows the epidemiological coverage formula for an imperfect vaccine, including an efficacy below which no coverage is enough. A tax on aggressive adaptation closes the gap between private and social incentives, and without it every market of two or more firms over-adapts. We confirm the base law in one measured check, which reruns an order-flow market simulator and reproduces its published amplification to every reported digit. Every other check of the theory confirms a formula against the model it came from, and is labeled that way. A pilot on 79 deployed language models puts the shared fraction near 0.43 on an error-correlation proxy. Ultimately, the framework gives a supervisor one market-level quantity and three instruments that act on it.
